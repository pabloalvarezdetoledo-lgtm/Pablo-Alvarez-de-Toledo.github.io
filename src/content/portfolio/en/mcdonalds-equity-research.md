## About this entry

This project is an independent, academic-style equity research exercise on McDonald's Corporation, the US-listed quick-service restaurant franchisor. It examines whether the roughly 20% decline in the share price from its highs reflects a genuine deterioration in the underlying business or is largely explained by the mechanical repricing of a defensive, dividend-paying stock as risk-free rates normalise from the depressed levels of the 2010s.

The analysis triangulates a twelve-month target price from a discounted free-cash-flow-to-firm model and forward P/E and EV/EBITDA multiples benchmarked against a peer group of listed restaurant franchisors, cross-checked with bear/base/bull scenarios and a sensitivity grid on the discount rate and terminal growth assumption. A separate qualitative section applies a capital-cycle framework, drawn from the Austrian tradition of capital theory, as an explicit stress test of the consensus bull case — used only to weight the downside scenario and never to alter a single input of the financial model.

Like the other case studies in this portfolio, this is an independent academic project completed outside any employer relationship. It is not an official research product of any institution, and the target price and hold-type recommendation it derives describe the output of the exercise's methodology rather than investment advice offered to readers.
